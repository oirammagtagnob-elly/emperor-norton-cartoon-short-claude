In eighteen fifty-nine, a bankrupt man declared himself Emperor of the United States. And a whole city went along with it.

San Francisco. Joshua Norton had just lost his fortune on a bad rice deal. So he walked into a newspaper office and handed over a proclamation. From now on, he said, he was Emperor.

The editors printed it as a joke. Then the joke refused to die. Norton put on a blue uniform and a feathered hat, and strolled the streets inspecting his empire.

His first decree abolished Congress. Congress didn't notice. So he printed his own money, and restaurants actually accepted it. Cafés hung brass plaques: "By appointment to the Emperor." Even the police saluted. The census listed his job as Emperor.

Why? Because he never hurt anyone. He only wanted dinner, and a little respect.

In eighteen eighty, he collapsed on a rainy street. The newspaper headline read: "The King Is Dead." Thousands lined the road.

Congress never obeyed a single order. But San Francisco gave him an emperor's funeral.
